# Stage 2 — Positioning (own wording of published frameworks: Dunford components, Golden Circle)

## Build in this order
1. **Competitive alternatives** — what the audience does today without this brand (including "do nothing" and "do it themselves").
2. **Unique attributes** — what this brand has that alternatives lack. Facts, not adjectives.
3. **Value** — what those attributes let the audience achieve. Phrase it as an outcome the audience would say.
4. **Best-fit audience** — who cares most; who is explicitly not the audience.
5. **Market frame** — the category that makes the value obvious ("documentary studio for regenerative stories" vs "video production").
6. **Why / how / what** (Golden Circle) — belief, method, offer. The belief is the source of voice; the method is the source of proof.

## Positioning statement (write 2-3 routes if the material supports it, then recommend one)
`For [best-fit audience] who [need/context], [Brand] is the [frame] that [value]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator], proven by [proof].`
Then a plain-language version of ≤20 words that someone could say aloud.

## Also produce
- Promise (one sentence, testable) and 3-5 values, each with a behaviour ("we do X even when Y").
- Proof points list: claim · evidence · source tag. No evidence = not in the book.
- Anti-positioning: three things the brand is not.

## Brand architecture (two linked brands, e.g. studio + person)
Pick one model and justify it: **monolith** (one name), **endorsed** (A, by B), **linked pair** (two names, one shared core). Write: what is shared (belief, values, proof), what differs (audience, tone range, visual accent), when each name is used, how they refer to each other. Default for a person plus their studio: linked pair with a shared core and distinct accent.
